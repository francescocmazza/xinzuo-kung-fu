# Sharpening section

This section teaches hand sharpening in two levels. The first chapter must help a reader complete a successful first sharpening with one simple, repeatable method. It may favour consistency over maximum efficiency: a beginner who works the full edge on every pass is more likely to obtain an even result, although a skilled sharpener would correct some areas locally.

For ordinary double-bevel knives, the beginner method sharpens only the narrow terminal microbevel. Never describe the broad primary bevel as the routine sharpening surface. Working that broad bevel changes blade geometry and belongs to thinning or advanced correction.

The second chapter introduces observation, pressure control, local correction, geometry and specialised knives only after the reader passes the practical tests in the first chapter. Preserve the principle behind the advanced material without forcing a beginner to make decisions they cannot yet evaluate.

Use short instructions, concrete comparisons and workshop examples. Every test must tell the reader what success feels or looks like and what to change when it fails. Follow the manuscript-wide sentence and sourcing rules in `EDITORIAL_REQUIREMENTS.md`; English remains the source of truth.
