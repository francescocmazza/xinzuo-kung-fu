# Preface

Every company develops knowledge. It grows through experience, through questions, through mistakes corrected and solutions repeated. Yet that knowledge does not always grow in an orderly way. One person may understand steel, another may know how a particular blade behaves, and someone else may have learned how to explain the difference between two knives to a customer. Unless these ideas are collected and shared, part of what the company knows remains scattered among individuals.

The idea for this book began inside Xinzuo with a very practical need: to consolidate our know-how and create a clear, dependable foundation for training our staff.

When a new colleague asks why a gyuto feels different from a Western chef's knife, why one steel is harder to sharpen than another, or whether a Damascus pattern changes cutting performance, the answer should not depend on who happens to be available that day. Our team needs a common language: accurate enough to be trusted, simple enough to be remembered and practical enough to be demonstrated with a knife and an ingredient.

My name is **Francesco Claudio Mazza**, and I am **EU Brand and Operations Manager at Xinzuo**. My work brings together the technical world of the product, the people who make and develop our knives, the staff who present them and the cooks and customers who use them. This position has shown me that even a beautifully made tool cannot explain itself. Its design, materials and intended movements have to be translated into knowledge that people can understand and use.

## From an internal manual to a book for everyone

At first, I imagined a training manual for the company. As the project developed, however, its purpose became broader. The same questions asked by a new member of staff are often the questions asked by someone buying a serious kitchen knife for the first time. The same explanation that helps a colleague recommend the right blade can help a home cook work more safely, sharpen with more confidence and understand what they are paying for.

The internal manual therefore became a book for everyone.

## Knowledge grows when it is shared

At Xinzuo, we believe that knowledge should be shared with as many people as possible. We do not grow by protecting useful ideas behind technical language or keeping experience inside a small circle. We grow by sharing what we know, listening to what others have learned and remaining willing to correct, refine and improve our understanding. In our view, the only lasting way to progress is to continue learning—and to help other people learn with us.

That belief is also part of the meaning of *kung fu* in this book. Mastery is not a secret possessed by a few people. It is knowledge made real through attention, effort and practice. A precise cut, a stable grip or a cleanly sharpened edge may look effortless, but each is the result of understanding repeated until it becomes skill.

> Knowledge becomes useful when it leaves the page, enters the hand and can be shared with someone else.

This guide is not intended to be a catalogue disguised as a book, nor is it a claim that there is one perfect knife for every person. Xinzuo provides the examples and the manufacturing experience, while the principles are meant to remain useful across the wider world of kitchen knives. My aim is to help the reader ask better questions, recognize meaningful differences and connect every technical idea to something that can be felt at the cutting board.

For Xinzuo staff, I hope these pages become a shared foundation from which better demonstrations, explanations and recommendations can grow. For cooks, enthusiasts and first-time buyers, I hope they remove some of the mystery from a tool that deserves to be understood as well as admired.

Most of all, I hope this book remains open in spirit. Knowledge is never truly finished. Materials evolve, techniques improve and every careful user can notice something worth sharing. This guide records what we have learned so far and invites the reader to continue the process.

## A book that can continue to improve

This book is also available free of charge on GitHub at:

**https://github.com/francescocmazza/xinzuo-kung-fu**

GitHub is not an intuitive tool for every reader. It was created for managing collaborative projects, and its language of repositories, issues and proposed changes can feel unfamiliar at first. That same structure, however, makes it particularly useful for keeping a work in progress transparent: every edition can be traced, corrections can be discussed and contributions can be incorporated without losing the history of the book.

Every contribution is welcome. If you notice an error, disagree with an explanation, know a clearer example or would like to propose an improvement, you can open a discussion or propose a correction through the GitHub repository.

You do not need to learn GitHub in order to take part. Comments, opinions, advice and proposed corrections can also be sent directly to **francescoclaudiomazza@gmail.com**. A short message identifying the chapter or passage is enough to begin the conversation.

This invitation is part of the purpose of the book. Calling the guide a work in progress does not mean accepting avoidable errors. It means recognizing that useful knowledge becomes stronger when readers test it, question it and help make the next edition clearer and more accurate than the one before it.

That is why I wrote it.
