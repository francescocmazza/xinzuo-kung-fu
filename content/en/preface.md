# Preface

The title of this book comes from an idea much broader than martial arts. *Gongfu* can describe a skill built through time, study, repetition and disciplined practice: knowledge turned into reliable ability.

That is the spirit of this book. A kitchen knife looks simple, but using one well takes more than learning a few movements. Skill grows faster when practice is supported by understanding: when the user knows why a knife behaves as it does, can separate meaningful differences from superficial ones and can connect what is felt in the hand with the choices behind the tool.

This book provides a technical and practical foundation for developing that competence. It cannot replace years of professional experience, and reading alone cannot create mastery. Its purpose is to make practice more effective: to help the reader observe carefully, understand established solutions, ask better questions and learn faster from experience. *Gongfu* begins by learning what others have discovered, then practising until those principles can be understood, judged, adapted and eventually improved.

The book combines personal study and experience with knowledge drawn from Xinzuo's work in product development, manufacturing, testing and direct contact with professional and home users. This practical perspective allows general principles of knife design and use to be examined through real decisions about materials, processes, geometry and construction. The aim is not simply to describe Xinzuo knives, but to use them as concrete examples through which the reader can develop the judgement needed to choose, use and care for a kitchen knife well.

## Why I wrote this book

This project began inside Xinzuo from a practical company need: **to train the people who work with our knives**. Knowledge about steels, heat treatment, blade geometry, construction, sharpening, use and maintenance existed across different people and different parts of the company, but it was not always collected in one place. A colleague learning how to explain, recommend or support a product should have access to the same dependable technical foundation regardless of who happens to be available to teach them that day.

The first purpose of the book is therefore still the one that gave birth to it. **It is intended to be a principal training guide for Xinzuo staff and for people who need to understand these products professionally.** It should provide a shared language and a practical body of knowledge that can support product training, demonstrations, customer advice and day-to-day work.

As the material grew, however, another point became increasingly clear: the questions asked by a new member of staff are often the same questions asked by cooks, enthusiasts, students, customers and people who are simply curious about knives. Why does one steel behave differently from another? What does forging really mean? Why does balance change with the grip? Why are two apparently similar knives designed differently? These are not questions that belong only to people who work in the knife industry.

That realization did not replace the original training purpose; it added a second one. **This book is also intended to make the world of kitchen knives easier to understand for a much wider public, including readers with no professional connection to the sector.** It aims to share technical knowledge in a form that remains rigorous enough for training while being clear and useful to anyone who wants to understand the materials, manufacturing choices, geometry, sharpening, use and care behind a good kitchen knife.

The book therefore has two connected roles: **a working foundation for professional training and a public guide for learning and dissemination**. The same explanation should help a colleague advise a customer more accurately and help an interested reader make a more informed choice, use a knife more safely or simply understand a subject they find fascinating.

## Why this book is about Xinzuo

There is also a personal reason Xinzuo became the frame of reference for this book. When I first came to know the company, what interested me was not a single model or specification, but the way product development connects materials, manufacturing processes and design.

That work extends far beyond designing a blade shape or choosing an attractive finish. Xinzuo works on steels, heat treatment, powder metallurgy, layered constructions, handle materials, balance, ergonomics, grinding, sharpening and testing. It uses established specialist steels when they are the right solution, but it also develops proprietary metallurgy such as 14Cr14MoVNb with industrial and academic partners. Selected series use demanding processes such as vacuum heat treatment and deep-freeze processing, while water-cooled grinding and whetstone sharpening protect the heat-treated edge during final manufacturing. Different steels, constructions, handle materials and balance strategies are used because different cooks, grips and tasks require different compromises.

That combination of manufacturing experience and continued experimentation shaped the way I learned to think about knives. Established solutions are respected, but materials, processes and designs are still examined against the question of whether they can be improved. Much of the knowledge collected in this book grew from working in that environment and from trying to make those decisions understandable to others.

Knowledge improves when it is shared. This book is also available free of charge on GitHub at **https://github.com/francescocmazza/xinzuo-kung-fu**. The repository supports collaborative revision, and corrections, comments and proposals are welcome there or by email at **francescoclaudiomazza@gmail.com**.

This book is a starting point: knowledge becomes practice, practice becomes experience, and experience can become *gongfu*.
