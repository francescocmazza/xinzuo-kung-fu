# In-store knife-selection consultation

Follow these six stages in this order.

1. **Handle preference:** ask whether the customer prefers an octagonal Asian-style handle or a classic Western-style handle.
2. **Natural grip:** observe their normal full-handle, intermediate/hybrid, or pinch grip.
3. **Physical ergonomic trial:** let them compare heavier and lighter sample knives, and then slightly blade-forward versus more centrally balanced knives, using their real grip. Weight and balance are two distinct preferences. The best knife should feel like a natural extension of the arm. Choosing a knife is like trying on shoes: each customer must find their individual fit.
4. **Food and blade geometry:** only now ask what they cut most: hard/dense, ordinary or soft vegetables, meat, or fish. Ask about movements and frequency to narrow down blade shape and ensure safe task suitability.
5. **Steel:** explain steel properties, sharpening and maintenance based on the actual tasks.
6. **Final model:** recommend a few knives matching ergonomics, use and the customer's aesthetic and budget preferences.

Do not reverse the order by beginning with steel or food type. Handle and grip first, then a weight-and-balance trial, then task and steel.
